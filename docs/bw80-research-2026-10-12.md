# 80 MHz (VHT80) top rung — research, 2026-10-12

Question: can the link reach the 50 Mb/s video spec on an 80 MHz top rung,
and what would it take? Research only: no code, no bench run, no flight.
Every number below is either quoted from a measured source (named) or
labelled as an estimate.

## Answer

Probably reachable on **VHT MCS4/80 (16-QAM 3/4, 175.5 Mb/s nominal)**, but
it hinges on one unmeasured number: the delivered fraction of nominal at
80 MHz. 50 Mb/s at the current settings (airtime budget 0.60, FEC 0.5/0.25,
share-weighted factor 1.40) needs 50 × 1.40 / 0.60 = **117 Mb/s delivered**,
i.e. MCS4/80 must deliver **≥ 67 % of nominal**. HT40 delivers 74–77 % with
agg6 (`docs/findings/2026-09-23-bw40-sweep.md`), but an 80 MHz PPDU is half
as long on air again, so the fixed per-PPDU cost weighs twice as much — and
HT40 singles already fell to 44–49 % at mcs5/7 for that reason. If the bench
says < 67 %, the levers are a larger aggregate (bigger loss unit), a lower
top-rung FEC (0.35/0.20 → needs ≥ 61 %), a higher top-rung budget (0.65 →
≥ 62 %), or MCS5/80 (64-QAM again, needs only ≥ 50 %).

Counterpart: at budget 0.60 each frame still spends ~60 % of the frame
interval on air (`docs/airtime-model.md` §8), so 80 MHz at 50 Mb/s buys
bitrate, not shorter frames — the size-driven latency spikes stay as they
are today.

## What the hardware and devourer already do

- Both ends are RTL8812EU (devourer Jaguar3 / rtl8822e; `docs/deploy.md`).
  devourer: "80 MHz works, incl. a 40-in-80 frame via TX-descriptor DATA_SC"
  (`devourer/src/jaguar3/CLAUDE.md`).
- **Primary must be the lowest 20 MHz of the block.** `central_and_pri()`
  hardcodes the 80 MHz primary to VHT_DATA_SC_20_LOWEST_OF_80MHZ (pri 4);
  the 40-in-80 frame lands on the LOWER 40 (DATA_SC 10). So the channel set
  at 80 MHz can only be {36, 52, 100, 116, 132, 149}, and the 20/40 rungs
  ride 36 / 36+40-style (HT40+, `ht40_offset` 1) inside the block. Other
  primary positions = a devourer change plus a kernel-parity check.
- **20-in-80 TX is not explicitly validated** on Jaguar3 (only 40-in-80 sets
  DATA_SC; a 20 MHz frame leaves it 0). Today's 20 MHz rungs and control
  frames already fly with DATA_SC 0 on a 40-tuned drone, so it probably
  lands on the primary the same way — bench item B2.
- **Spur blocks.** The 8822E spur table covers 80 MHz centres 58, 106, 122,
  155 (and 171): those blocks get an NBI notch + CSI mask and FastRetune
  declines every hop into or out of them (full retune; 65–91 ms at 40 MHz,
  unmeasured at 80). **Spur-free 80 MHz blocks: 36–48 and 132–144 only.**
  36–48 is where the operator's home Wi-Fi sits; 132–144 is DFS (the caller
  owns compliance — devourer and mabur enforce none).
- **A wide receiver hears narrow frames.** devourer measured an 80-tuned
  receiver decoding 40 and 20 MHz frames on its primary without retuning
  (`devourer/tests/rx80_narrow_tx_probe.sh`) at a wide-RX penalty of 0 dB
  (≤ 1 dB, `tests/wide_rx_penalty_sweep.sh`). Counterpart: that was an
  8822BU → 8814AU pair, not the 8812EU; mabur's own 40-vs-20 tune on the
  8812EU GS cards cost 0.8–1.1 dB (`bw40-sweep-findings` "wide RX").
- **One dirty slice kills the 80 MHz frame.** devourer's jammed-slice test
  (`docs/pseudo-preamble-puncturing.md`): one jammed 20 MHz quarter cut
  VHT80 delivery to 50 %; dropping to the clean 40 recovered 78 % of clean.
  The ladder's 40 rungs are that fallback, but only for the LOWER 40.
- **mabur plumbing already present:** the profile byte has a VHT bit and an
  80 MHz bw code (`common/src/profile.cpp`), `phy_rate_mbps` has the VHT80
  table, and the drone's `to_tx_mode` passes VHT + bw to devourer's radiotap
  builder (`drone/src/radio_tx.cpp`).

## Other ceilings near 50 Mb/s

| ceiling | evidence | at 50 Mb/s |
|---|---|---|
| drone CPU | flight 0027: 47 % median (57 % max) of both cores at 24 Mb/s, ~1.1 points per Mb/s; bench 2026-09-22: 22 Mb/s clean at 59 %, extrapolated wall 45–50 Mb/s (`bitrate-ceiling-findings-2026-09-21.md`) | ~77 % median, extrapolated. The hot path is one thread on one core, which can saturate before the total does. Today's 35 Mb/s flight is the first point above 24. |
| drone USB to the 8812EU | 40 MHz bench carried ~100 Mb/s of air through it (MCS7/40 agg6) | needs ~70 Mb/s of bodies (50 × 1.40) — inside what was measured |
| encoder | never run above ~24 Mb/s in flight | unknown at 1080p60 50 Mb/s |
| range | 802.11 minimum sensitivities: MCS4/80 −64 dBm vs MCS5/40 −63 dBm | MCS4/80 ≈ MCS5/40 reach (spec estimate, not measured), and needs −19 dB EVM vs −22 dB |

## Plan

**A. Bench tool (small, bench-only code).** Extend `bench/linkbench`:
`--bw 80` on tx and rx (tune `SelectedChannel{ch, 0, CHANNEL_WIDTH_80}`,
`ch` the lowest of the block), VHT radiotap for 80 MHz frames,
`--frame-bw 20|40|80|mix`, and the `--range-sweep` bw mask. Get
`linkbench-tx` onto the drone: no ARM toolchain in the cloud session, so
either a bench firmware build with `-DMABUR_BUILD_LINKBENCH=ON`
(openipc-builder; check NOR space) or `tools/build-arm.sh` on a host that
has the OpenIPC toolchain.

**A is done (2026-10-14).** `linkbench-tx` and `linkbench-rx` take
`--bw 80` (channel = the block-lowest 20: 36 52 100 116 132 149, checked
by `mabur::vht80_block_lowest`). 80 MHz frames are VHT 1SS (MCS 0..9);
20/40 frames in an 80-tuned run stay HT unless `--vht`. New:
`--frame-bw 80`, `--frame-bw mix` cycling 20/40/80 at `--bw 80`,
`--range-bw-mask` bit 2 (80 MHz rows, MCS8/9 via `--range-mcs-mask 0x3ff`),
and `linkbench-rx --rate-hist` "B parity rate_code bw count" lines with the
raw RX rate code and width (0x0C+n = HT MCSn, 0x2C+n = VHT MCSn; bw 0/1/2
= 20/40/80), so a run shows what actually aired. Both binaries ship in the
d7 drone / gs7 GS images (`/usr/bin`). How to run them, on 36 (spur-free;
swap in 132 for the second block):

```sh
# both ends: stop the daemons first, restore with `start` after
ssh drone '/etc/init.d/S00mabur stop'
ssh gs    '/etc/init.d/S96maburgs stop'
# B1 capacity, MCS4/80 (repeat for --mcs 3 and 5, and --ampdu 12)
ssh gs    'linkbench-rx --channel 36 --bw 80 --index 0 --symbol-size 332 --bpb 4 --window 32 --overhead 0.5 --time 25'
ssh drone 'linkbench-tx --channel 36 --bw 80 --mcs 4 --ldpc --stbc --ampdu 6 --pwr-mode none \
           --symbol-size 332 --bpb 4 --window 32 --overhead 0.5 --bitrate 90M --time 17'
# B2 20/40-in-80: every B line should show all three widths
ssh gs    'linkbench-rx --channel 36 --bw 80 --index 0 --rate-hist --time 25'
ssh drone 'linkbench-tx --channel 36 --bw 80 --frame-bw mix --mcs 3 --ldpc --stbc --bitrate 10M --time 17'
# B3+B4 range and wide-RX penalty: card 0 at 80, card 1 at 40 (hears the 20/40 rows)
ssh gs    'linkbench-rx --channel 36 --bw 80 --index 0 --range /tmp/r80.txt & \
           linkbench-rx --channel 36 --bw 40 --index 1 --range /tmp/r40.txt'
ssh drone 'linkbench-tx --channel 36 --bw 80 --range-sweep --range-bw-mask 7 --range-mcs-mask 0x38 --ldpc --stbc --time 600'
```

Read B1 as the 40 MHz table was read: median steady-second "air Mbps", ×
(1 − crc_bad/frames), divided by 175.5 (MCS4/80 nominal). The trap: the
drone carried ~100 Mb/s of air at 40 MHz and was never pushed further, so
a flat ~100 across MCS3/4/5 at 80 MHz means the drone's USB or CPU capped
it, not the air -- run MCS3 (117 nominal) and MCS5 (234) beside MCS4
before calling a number. A lower `--overhead` cuts the drone's FEC work
for the same air if that cap shows.

**B. Bench measurements (decide go / no-go).**
1. Delivered fraction at VHT MCS3/4/5 80 MHz, agg6 and a larger aggregate,
   on 132 and 36 → `air_clock.efficiency_80`. Go needs MCS4/80 ≥ ~0.67 (or
   one of the levers above).
2. 20-in-80 and 40-in-80 from the drone's 8812EU into an 80-tuned 8812EU GS
   card (control frames and the 20/40 rungs depend on it).
3. Wide-RX penalty on the 8812EU: 20 and 40 MHz frames received at an
   80 vs a 40 tune (the 20/40 rungs pay it).
4. Range: MCS4/80 vs MCS5/40 and MCS4/40 (`--range-sweep`, `rangean.py`).
5. `maburcal` at 80 MHz (64-QAM-free, but the walls are per rate and width).
6. Drone CPU and encoder at 50 Mb/s on a pinned rung (needs C's static pin).

**C. Flight code (only after B says go).**
- Config: `radio.width = 80` (both ends), ladder `bw = 80` → a VHT rung,
  `air_clock.efficiency_80`, `ampdu.min_mcs_80`, `[[encoder.rung]] bw = 80`,
  `link.static_bw = 80`.
- Channel-set rule at 80: members are block-lowest channels sharing the
  80 MHz grid (`channel_set_issue`), the 40 rungs on the lower 40.
- Tuning: the drone's `InitWrite`, the GS's `RadioFrontend` InitWrite /
  `set_width` / `width_resync`, `RemoteCard::sec_for` (CPE510 relays are
  ath9k, 11n: they cannot receive an 80 MHz rung at all).
- Boot scout: score all four quarters of each block and block the block if
  any quarter is busy (the 40 MHz pair rule, extended). In-flight scout
  stays primary-only, so it is blind to three quarters of the block — a
  dirty upper half shows only as 80-rung probe failures.
- Observability: ctllog/fec/probe already carry `bw`; flightreport and the
  OSD need `/80`.
- Then a flight with the 80 rung on top of today's ladder.

## Not covered

DFS behaviour (radar detection is not implemented anywhere in this stack),
160 MHz, two spatial streams (the 8812EU TX is single-path 1SS,
`devourer/docs/8822e-quirks.md`), and other primary positions inside the block.
