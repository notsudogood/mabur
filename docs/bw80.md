# 80 MHz rungs

Since 2026-10-17 (gs8 / d8) the ladder tops out at 80 MHz: VHT MCS3/80 and
MCS4/80 above HT MCS4/40. Research: `docs/bw80-research-2026-10-12.md`.
Bench: `docs/findings/2026-10-17-bw80-bench.md`. Unflown at the time of
writing.

## Config (both ends)

- `radio.width = 80` on the drone and the GS: the widest rung. Since d11
  the drone comes up at `radio.home_width` (40) and steps its radio to the
  flown or probed rung's width with `radio.width_step` (below); d8–d10
  tuned VHT80 at bring-up and kept it. A drone at 20/40 told to fly an
  80 MHz rung flies it at 40, HT, and logs it once.
- `radio.channels`: since gs10/d11 every member is any channel of an 80 MHz
  block (36–48, 52–64, 100–112, 116–128, 132–144, 149–161, 165–177, and on
  in blocks of 16 up to devourer's tune limit, 233), all on the same side of
  their 40 MHz pair; the channel is the primary and devourer tunes the
  block centre. Default `[36, 132]`, the two blocks without an 8812EU
  spur. 52–144 are DFS and 165 up past UNII-3: the operator owns that call.
- Drone `[[encoder.rung]]`: 25 Mb/s at MCS4/40, 35 at MCS3/80, 50 at MCS4/80.
  `ampdu.min_mcs_80` and `air_clock.efficiency_80` cover the 80 MHz rungs.
- GS ladder (`gs/bundle/maburgs.default.toml`): 0–4/20, 3–4/40, 3–4/80.
  The ladder may hold up to 10 rungs.
- `power_mode = "offset"` now shapes the 80 MHz rungs too: devourer gives
  VHT 1SS MCS0–7 the HT MCS0–7 diffs and MCS8/9 MCS7's (d11). The walls
  were measured on HT frames; a VHT frame's own wall is unmeasured.

## GS card widths

A card tuned 80 hears the 20/40 rungs ~1–1.5 dB worse (bench, up to ~3 on
one card). So at `radio.width = 80` the cards live at 40 (the primary's own
40 MHz pair) and widen only while it pays (`gs/src/width_policy.h`):

- an 80 MHz rung is flown: every card widens;
- an 80 MHz rung is probed (the 40 → 80 promote): one card widens, a non-TX
  card first, so the other keeps the uplink;
- each need is held 3 s after it was last seen, so probe pauses and
  promote/demote bounces do not retune each time.

With `radio.narrow_20` (gs10, on in the bundle) the same rule reaches one
step down: on the 20 MHz rungs every card sits at 20 (the SNR readout was
0.8–1.1 dB better tuned 20 than 40, near its ceiling), and the 20 → 40
probe widens one card first.

Switches run one card at a time on a worker thread, at least 250 ms apart
(each is devourer's `FastSetChannel`: the full channel set, batched with
`radio.batched_tune` -- ~60 USB round trips instead of ~190 on the 8822E,
parity-proven offline, wall-clock on the GS host unmeasured), and never while that card is in an in-flight dwell;
in-flight dwells pause while a card is wide for a probe. A relay (CPE510,
11n) never widens. Each switch logs
`maburgs radio: card N width -> W MHz on ch C ok|FAILED in T ms (reason)`.

The web GS tunes 20 or 40 only: it leaves the 80 MHz rungs out.

## Drone width steps (d11, backlog item 14)

`radio.width_step = true` (bundle): the radio sits at `home_width` (40) and,
before an 80 MHz rung's (or 80 MHz probe's) frames are published, switches
to 80 on the same primary; it goes back after `width_hold_ms` (3 s) with no
80 MHz need. A wide receiver hears narrow frames, so the drone is never
narrower than what it sends. Each switch logs
`maburd: width A -> B MHz on ch C (why) in T ms: radio at central X, want Y`
and the stats line carries `w=` (tuned width), `wsw=` (switches) and
`wsw_fail=`. A switch that throws turns stepping off for the session and
parks the radio at `radio.width`, the d8–d10 behaviour. `false` = d8–d10.

Why: the drone's receiver hears the GS's 20 MHz control frames better at
40 than at 80 (~1–1.5 dB measured on the GS cards, the drone's own not
measured), and those frames are what the 3 s failsafe counts.

Measured (operator bench, d11, 2026-10-21, ch 132, 5 switches): every
switch landed on the right centre (138 at 80, 134 at 40) with the link up,
but each took **68–99 ms**, not the ~5 ms estimated from the batched
set's ~60 round trips. That is 4–6 frames at 60 fps per 40/80 crossing,
the same range as the plain full set on the gs8 GS cards (65–91 ms), so
the batching saves no time on this host and the cost sits somewhere the
offline round-trip count does not see. Not yet broken down; recalibration
is skipped, which may also cost EVM at 80.

### Breakdown and lean switch (d12 + gs11, a data build)

Every width switch, and every full-set hop, now logs a second line with
devourer's breakdown of that set (`LastChannelSetStats`):

    maburd:   set: batched 85.0 ms = lock 0.1 + prime 2.1 + batches 30.2 (12, 190 writes)
              + single 40.1 (45) + post 10.2 + other 2.5; xfers 260, dack loops 8

(GS: `maburgs radio: card N width C -> C set: ...`). `lock` is the wait
for devourer's register lock, `prime` its one batched read, `batches` the
write batches, `single` reads and writes that waited on their own round
trip (the DAC confirm loop and the CSI table port among them), `post` the
TX-power / CCA re-asserts after the set, `other` the rest (on the plain
path, the whole set). `dack loops` above 8 means the DAC write-confirm
retried.

`radio.lean_width` (both ends, default **true** after d16/gs15) uses devourer's lean
width switch: it writes only what the full set would change, learned from
the full sets this session ran, so the first switch to each width is
always the full set. Offline it leaves the chip byte-identical to the full
set in ~20 round trips instead of ~190 (~80 vs ~215 on a spur primary
such as 153) -- a count against a fake chip, not a time (the bench and
flight times are below). `radio.lean_verify` (default true) reads the changed registers back
and falls back to the full set on any difference; the line then ends
`lean declined: <why>` and each mismatch gets its own
`lean verify miss: reg 0x.... want .. got ..` line. Those lines are the
data: a register that never reads back what was written means the lean
switch cannot work as built on this radio.

Measured (operator bench, d12 + gs11, 2026-10-21, ch 132, batched, 40 <-> 80):

| End | Switches | Total | Write batches (29) | Single round trips (30) | Priming read |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| drone (SSC338Q) | 7 | 79-135 ms | 61-112 ms | 6-29 ms | 2.5-13.5 ms |
| GS cards (RK3566) | 16 | 25-36 ms | 12.5-19.6 ms | 8.6-13.6 ms | 2.7-4.8 ms |

Both ends did the same 171-173 control transfers; lock wait and the
re-asserts after the set were 0.0, and the DAC confirm loop never retried
(8). So the drone pays ~0.55 ms per transfer and the GS ~0.18, where
InitWrite on the same drone (a core to itself) measured 27 us per pipelined
write. Fewer transfers (the lean switch) can only cut the drone's time by
the ratio of the counts, ~3x; the per-transfer cost is the larger lever.
Leading explanation, not yet tested: CPU, not USB. Each completion needs
the RX thread (it holds libusb's event lock and reaps it) and then the
switching thread to run, both on cpu0 beside the encoder at SCHED_FIFO 50
and the FEC worker; the 2.5-13.5 ms spread of a single batched read fits
scheduling, not a bus. Counter-evidence it would not explain: the same
pattern from a slow host controller under RX bulk-IN load.

`radio.switch_rt_prio` (drone, d13; default 60 on the drone after d16, 0 =
off, and 0 on the GS) was the test: > 0 runs
the switching thread and the RX thread at that SCHED_FIFO priority for each
FastSetChannel / FastRetune (drone/src/rt_boost.h) and restores both after.
The width and retune lines end `[rt 60 x2]` when both were raised. If the
switch gets no faster with it, the time is in the USB host.

Measured (operator bench, d13 + gs11, 2026-10-22, ch 132, batched, 40 <-> 80,
`switch_rt_prio = 60`): 5 switches, **14.1-16.6 ms** (d12 without it:
79-135 ms), every one `(ok)` with both threads raised. Same 171-173
transfers: batches 7.5-8.2 ms, single round trips 3.6-4.7 ms, priming read
2.1-2.6 ms, so ~0.08 ms per transfer against ~0.55. It was the scheduler.
Counterpart: one drone, one session, five switches, and what the boost
does to the video around a switch (the encoder waits on cpu0 for the
switch's CPU slices) is not yet reported. The GS cards, unboosted, now
take longer (25-36 ms) than the drone.

With `lean_width = true` as well (same bench, G3): the first switch to each
width ran the full set (`lean declined: width not learned yet`, ~15 ms);
the next five were lean, **7.8-8.5 ms** per switch (the set itself 7.0-8.2),
85-87 transfers instead of 171-173, and the readback check found 0
mismatches (7 registers checked going up, 11 going down), every switch on
the right centre. Counterpart: five lean switches, one channel (132), 40
<-> 80 only; 20 MHz and a spur primary (153) have not run lean on a real
chip, and the check reads back only the registers the lean plan wrote.

Flown (drone 1, gs15/d16, 2026-10-26, ch 116, `wide_igi_floor = 53`,
drone `switch_rt_prio = 60` + `lean_width = true`): two climbs at full
rate went 40 -> 80 MHz (50.9 s and 116.2 s) without a latency spike. The
two flights before it, same drone without the two settings, spiked 64-83 ms
at the first 80 MHz test frame each time (42.7 s and 284.7 s), and once
dropped the video to 30 fps for ~2 s. That is why both are on by default
after d16 (lean on the GS too; its boost stays 0: the GS switches one card
at a time, so a switch never deafens it, and 60 would outrank the player's
frame-release thread at 10). Counterpart: one flight, mostly indoors and
close, 70 % of it in low power; the drone's own switch lines (times,
`lean verify miss`) from that flight were not collected, and the GS cards
have only run lean on the bench.

## Hops at 80 MHz (gs10/d11, backlog item 0)

A radio tuned 80, or one whose 40 MHz pair side changes, hops with the
full channel set (`FastSetChannel`) instead of `FastRetune`, on both ends:
the 80 MHz lean hop is the prime suspect for the after-hop deafness of
backlog item 0. `radio.fast_hop_at_80 = true` restores the lean hop. The
drone's retune line names the path (`FastSetChannel` / `FastRetune`) and
its time. A hop to a channel with no 80 MHz block steps the radio to its
home width.
